What is DoseCheck?

DoseCheck is a pharmaceutical technology project exploring how structured medication information, software engineering, natural language processing, and retrieval-based AI can support medication-safety review.

The project is being designed around potential problems such as:

💊 Potential drug-drug interactions
⚠️ Contraindication screening
📏 Dose-related risk identification
🧑‍⚕️ Patient-specific medication considerations
📄 Prescription interpretation
🔎 Evidence retrieval
🧠 Natural Language Processing
📚 Retrieval-Augmented Generation
📝 Explainable medication-safety information
🧪 System testing and evaluation
🧠 DoseCheck — Core Philosophy

The intended concept is not:

Prescription

AI

Prescription / Medication Information

The goal is to explore decision support, not automatic clinical decision-making.

🧠 Why DoseCheck?

Medication-related decisions can involve many variables:

Medication
    +
Dose
    +
Frequency
    +
Patient
    +
Age
    +
Medical Conditions
    +
Other Medications
    +
Allergies
    +
Clinical Context
    +
Evidence

A software system may potentially help organise and surface relevant information.

However:

A software output is not automatically a clinical conclusion.

DoseCheck is therefore intended to explore information organisation, risk flagging, evidence retrieval, and explainability, while keeping qualified healthcare professionals involved in actual medication-related decisions.

🧠 My Problem-Solving Framework

Before building something, I try to ask:

01 — Is this actually a problem?

Not every interesting idea is useful.

02 — Who has the problem?

A solution should have a clear user.

03 — Why does the problem exist?

Understanding the root cause matters more than immediately coding.

04 — Does technology actually help?

Not every problem requires AI.

05 — What information is needed?

Data quality matters.

06 — How will we test it?

A demonstration is not proof of reliability.

07 — How can it fail?

Failure analysis is part of engineering.

08 — Where must humans remain involved?

Especially in healthcare.
